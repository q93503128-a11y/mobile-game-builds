# Research — Commercial Inventory & Touch UX

Research was used to choose interaction principles, not to copy game layouts or assets. The decision rows below connect a concrete public example to a specific Phase 4B problem and show both adopted and rejected patterns.

| Game / source | System and verified example | Benefit in the source | Lowfire difference / current problem | Lowfire application | Decision and rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Core Keeper** — [inventory screenshot and annotated screen breakdown](https://playcraft.space/gallery/core-keeper/inventory-crafting-pouches-hotbar/), with the game’s [Inventory community wiki](https://core-keeper.fandom.com/wiki/Inventory) | A frame from the publisher-released *Bags and Blasts* trailer shows a crafting panel, a character/equipment figure, four pouch slots, a hotbar and a numbered backpack grid. The community wiki also documents compatible inventory items highlighting when an equipment slot is hovered. | Real item pictures, visible quantities, and a visible relationship between inventory and gear support recognition and equipment selection. | Phase 4B had a horizontal four-row equipment rail: it read like settings, emphasized text strips, and separated equipment meaning from the item icons. Lowfire has four equipment kinds, not Core Keeper’s paper doll and pouch system. | Use a compact 2×2 **icon-first** group, category names, distinct selected/candidate states, and visible Equip/Unequip actions. Keep selection feedback on the matching slot. | Adopt the icon-first relationship and candidate feedback. Reject the paper doll, pouches, crafting panel layout, and item/equipment rules: they would add systems or copy another game’s screen. |
| **Necesse** — [official wiki: Inventory](https://necessewiki.com/Inventory), [official wiki: Controls](https://necessewiki.com/Controls) | The current wiki describes 40 inventory slots in a 4×10 grid plus a distinct 10-slot hotbar; it separately documents optional category pouches and expandable bags. | A predictable grid and separate action bar make slot location/count easy to learn. | Lowfire’s model is fixed at Storage 27 + Hotbar 9. It needs nine columns and three storage rows at the current reference layout. | Preserve the existing 9-column Storage/Hotbar division and improve count-label space without changing data. | Adopt grid regularity and separate banks. Reject pouches, Void Pouch/Bag expansion, and 4×10 reflow because Lowfire has no such data/feature and the 844×390 view is constrained. |
| **Terraria Mobile** — [official Terraria Wiki: Game controls](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_controls) and [505 Games mobile patch notes](https://support.505games.com/support/solutions/articles/150000146926-terraria-console-mobile-v1-4-3-patch-notes-2022-april-27-) | The mobile controls article documents tap-and-drag inventory moves, a scrollable inventory, an Equipment tab with Equip/Unequip actions, and touch controls that can be moved/adjusted. | Touch operations and explicit contextual actions reduce reliance on tiny pointer targets or hidden desktop-only behavior. | Lowfire already supports drag/drop, stack splitting, equipping, replacement, and unequipping. It must retain all of them without adding a tab maze to one landscape screen. | Keep direct slot interaction and explicit Equip/Unequip controls; make the selected/candidate/empty states visible. | Adopt explicit action/state labels and direct touch targets. Reject copying Terraria’s five-part mobile HUD, exact tab arrangement, or its item behavior. |
| **Unity UI/game design** — [Unity: How to immerse your players through effective UI and game design](https://unity.com/blog/games/how-to-immerse-your-players-through-effective-ui-and-game-design) | Unity’s game UI guidance says interface function should fit the game’s art direction and identity, and calls out distracting or misplaced pop-ups as immersion risks. | A coherent game identity can keep necessary UI readable without making every surface compete with gameplay. | Lowfire is a top-down 2D survival game with its own sprite outlines and warm earth palette. Phase 4B frames used repeated bevels and dominant rails; translucent modal layers let the underlying grid compete with modal text. | Keep the Lowfire world and item art; use subdued flat brown 2D surfaces, thinner 9-slice edges, and opaque modal backing. | Adopt restraint and context. Reject large ornamental wood/metal panels, copied game skins, and replacing the world with a UI image. |
| **Android accessibility** — [Android Developers: Make apps more accessible (Views)](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/views/apps-views) | Android recommends a focusable/touch target of at least 48×48 dp. | Gives a platform-specific minimum touch-area reference, including padding beyond the visible artwork. | Unity Game View screenshots are physical editor pixels at requested dimensions. Canvas units and device density determine the eventual Android dp size. | Use generous non-overlapping slot/button hit areas in reference layout and keep the measurement requirement for Android device QA. | Adopt 48 dp as a later device acceptance criterion. Do **not** claim 844 screenshot pixels or RectTransform units equal dp. |
| **Apple Game Controls HIG** — [Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls) | Apple recommends frequent touch controls at least 44×44 pt, less important controls at least 28×28 pt, safe-area-aware placement, and visible/tactile pressed feedback. | Reachable, sufficiently large controls and visible response make direct interaction easier. | Lowfire is a landscape Unity game and this run had no iPhone/iPad device measurement. | Keep the cell hit target larger than its visible icon/frame, expose clear selected feedback, and keep safe area/device review as a release check. | Adopt as a cross-check. Reject direct conversion from Unity screenshot pixels to Apple points. |
| **Nielsen Norman Group** — [10 Usability Heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) | Visibility of system status, recognition rather than recall, and aesthetic/minimalist design each recommend visible state and low competition from irrelevant information. | Players can recognize the current item/action and do not have to parse duplicate labels. | Phase 4B repeated “equipped” status on all four occupied equipment slots; the item's icon already represented the equipped item. | Show current equipped state through item-in-equipment placement; show a text state on the selected equipped cell, and retain text for empty/candidate states. | Adopt state feedback and remove redundant copy. Keep details/actions available on selection rather than removing functionality. |
| **Parhi, Karlson & Bederson (MobileHCI 2006)** — [Target Size Study for One-Handed Thumb Use on Small Touchscreen Devices](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2006/01/parhi-mobileHCI06.pdf) | The study reports 9.2 mm for discrete targets and 9.6 mm for serial targets as sizes that should be sufficient for one-handed thumb interaction in the tested small touchscreen setup. | Provides empirical evidence that target size and thumb movement affect mobile interaction. | The study is older and its device/task setup is not the same as current Android hardware or Lowfire’s landscape UI. | Avoid overlapping equipment cells and preserve larger touch areas behind visible slot art. | Use as supporting evidence only. Physical measurements and one-handed reach remain unverified until tested on target devices. |

## Resulting principles

1. Item icons and category labels carry the equipment identity; state text appears only when it resolves a meaningful difference (empty, candidate, or selected equipped slot).
2. Keep the existing inventory banks and direct item operations; do not add pouches, bags, tabs, or item rules for visual reasons.
3. Make the selected state, compatible candidate, and Equip/Unequip action readable without a repeated text rail.
4. Use the game’s actual world and item sprites with restrained flat 2D surfaces. The modal backdrop is opaque so inventory text does not bleed through.
5. Verify physical touch target dimensions on a real device; editor pixels are not dp or pt.
